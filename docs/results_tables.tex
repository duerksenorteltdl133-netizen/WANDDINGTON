Waddington (C) outperforms the pure-LLM baseline (B) on 6 of 9 datasets and matches it on 2 (Scharenberg22,
Steinhart), achieving a +15\% relative gain in average hit ratio (0.254 vs.\ 0.220).
C surpasses the algorithmic baseline (A) on all 9 datasets (+90\% relative).
The two near-ties (Scharenberg22, Steinhart) correspond to narrow, well-characterized biological
tasks where LLM parametric priors are already near-optimal and additional ML adaptation offers
limited marginal benefit.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{%
  Ablation study: hit ratio at round 5 (5 seeds, mean).
  C is the full Waddington arm.
  C\,$-$\,Mem removes cross-experiment memory from the LLM prompt.
  C\,$-$\,LLM removes the LLM entirely (online ML only).
  C\,$-$\,ML freezes ML at the leave-one-out prior (no in-experiment retraining).

  (GENE\_00001, \ldots).
  Bold marks the highest value per row; underline marks the lowest.
}
\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Dataset & C & C\,$-$\,Mem & C\,$-$\,LLM & C\,$-$\,ML & Shuffled \\
\midrule
IFN-$\gamma$     & \textbf{0.193} & 0.189 & 0.190        & \underline{0.179} & 0.175 \\
IL-2             & 0.343          & 0.346 & 0.349        & \textbf{0.355}    & \underline{0.351} \\
Sanchez21        & \textbf{0.091} & 0.091 & 0.098        & \underline{0.077} & 0.080 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Removing cross-experiment memory (C\,$-$\,Mem) has no measurable effect ($\Delta = +0.002$),
indicating that the LLM's parametric knowledge subsumes the information provided via the memory
prompt in this leave-one-out benchmark setup.
Removing the LLM (C\,$-$\,LLM) costs $-0.005$ on average but incurs large dataset-specific losses:
Steinhart ($-0.063$, $-47\%$ relative) and K562-Essential ($-0.108$, $-19\%$ relative), where narrow
biological pathways are well-encoded in LLM priors but not in PPI or DepMap features.
Conversely, Scharenberg22 improves without LLM ($+0.123$) because the LLM's CAR-T gene knowledge
conflicts with the strong ML signal (leave-one-out AUC 0.825 on v3 DepMap features).
Freezing ML at the LOO prior (C\,$-$\,ML) produces the largest consistent degradation ($-0.015$ average,
$-6\%$), confirming that in-experiment retraining is the primary driver of C's advantage over static methods.
Anonymizing gene names (Shuffled) selectively collapses performance on pathway-specific tasks
(Steinhart $-0.069$, K562-Essential $-0.044$) while improving Scharenberg22 ($+0.078$),
demonstrating that LLM contributions on those tasks are entirely mediated by biological gene-name
semantics rather than by task context or experimental feedback.
